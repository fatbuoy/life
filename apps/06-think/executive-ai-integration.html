Eight Use Cases
How Senior Executives Are Using AI
01 · Foundation

AI-Assisted Executive Communication

Senior leaders use AI to adapt a single core message across multiple audiences — board pack, all-hands, analyst call, press release — automatically adjusting tone, depth, and framing. AI also serves as a rehearsal partner: simulate the negotiation, surface the objections, prepare the responses before the room.

Low complexity · High daily leverage
Source: BCG CEO Survey 20261
02 · Foundation

Meeting Preparation & Follow-through

AI agents that surface context, pre-brief agendas, take structured notes, assign follow-ups, and chase owners. What was once a chief of staff function is increasingly automated. BCG notes that the most effective CEOs treat AI as an orchestration layer across their direct reports — not just a personal productivity tool.1

Low complexity · Immediate time return
Source: BCG, 20261
03 · Intelligence

Deep Intelligence Synthesis

Executives use AI to compile competitive signals, earnings summaries, regulatory shifts, and market movements into a synthesized brief — the equivalent of a permanent analyst team on call. McKinsey found that AI high performers saved over 1.5 million hours in search-and-synthesis work annually.3 The step change is moving from summarisation to synthesis with strategic context.

Moderate complexity · High strategic leverage
Source: McKinsey State of AI 20253
04 · Intelligence

Red-Teaming & Decision Stress-Testing

Rather than seeking validation, leading executives use AI to actively challenge their thinking — surfacing the strongest case against their strategy, identifying blind spots, and running structured scenario analysis. Critically, AI has no political stake in the outcome. IBM found that over two-thirds of surveyed executives cite improved decision-making as the primary value of AI systems.6

Moderate complexity · High strategic value
Source: IBM Institute for Business Value, 20256
05 · Execution ★ You Are Here

Research → Plan → Build (Vibe Coding)

Synthesising multiple inputs into a clear brief, developing a sequenced plan through AI challenge, then using one-prompt AI to build working prototypes. This is the practice you have already developed — and it sits ahead of most executive peers. McKinsey data shows only 21% of companies have fundamentally redesigned any workflow around AI.3

Your current position on the scale
Source: McKinsey State of AI 20253
06 · Execution

AI-Driven Workforce & Talent Planning

AI is being applied to traditionally human-judgment territory: org design, succession planning, and capability gap analysis. Feed in role data, performance signals, and strategic roadmaps — AI surfaces talent risks before they become crises. PwC estimates AI agents can perform approximately half of tasks humans currently do in complex workflows.2

Higher complexity · Long-term org impact
Source: PwC AI Predictions 20262
07 · Frontier

Agentic Workflow Delegation

The shift from AI that advises to AI that acts. Agents monitor pipelines, trigger alerts, execute multi-step tasks, and coordinate cross-functional data gathering without human intermediaries. McKinsey's 25,000 AI agents are already deployed inside the firm itself.3 PwC notes agentic workflows are spreading faster than governance models can address their needs.2

High complexity · Transformative ROI
Source: PwC 20262 · McKinsey 20253
08 · Frontier

End-to-End Process Reinvention

The defining differentiator between AI leaders and laggards. Not deploying AI into existing workflows, but redesigning workflows entirely around AI's capabilities. BCG found that AI front-runners are twice as likely to deploy agents end-to-end across a full process. Deloitte reports only 34% of companies are genuinely reimagining their business rather than optimising it.4

Highest complexity · Competitive moat
Source: BCG 20261 · Deloitte 20264

What's Next
The Leading Edge

Where the Frontier Executives Are Going

The gap between Position 5 and the frontier is not about new tools — it is about two structural shifts in how AI is used.

→

From assisted to delegated. The frontier moves from AI that informs decisions to AI that executes on them. BCG found that AI leaders direct more than half of their 2026 AI investments toward end-to-end agentic workflows — systems that monitor, decide, and act with defined boundaries and oversight rather than waiting for the next prompt.1

→

From personal practice to organisational capability. The most important shift for an executive at your level is not deepening personal AI use — it is embedding that thinking discipline into how your team operates. McKinsey identifies the single strongest predictor of enterprise AI impact as whether an organisation fundamentally redesigned its workflows. The executives driving this are doing so top-down.3

→

From workflow optimisation to workflow reinvention. Deloitte's 2026 survey finds that advanced organisations are not improving existing processes with AI — they are eliminating the process architecture entirely and rebuilding around what AI makes possible. Only 34% of companies are doing this.4

→

AI as a learning accelerator — at scale. Harvard Business School research identifies leaders using AI as a strategic partner for reasoning, not just analysis — actively prompting AI to challenge mental models, expose strategic blind spots, and stress-test assumptions before committing capital or organisational change.5

Summary Observation
The Honest Assessment

What the Data Says

The BCG, McKinsey, and Deloitte surveys consistently point to the same finding: the executives creating real competitive advantage from AI are not those with the most sophisticated tools — they are those who have fundamentally redesigned how work is conceived and executed.1,3,4 Most organisations are still at the stage of "adding AI to existing workflows." The minority are rebuilding the workflow around AI's capabilities.

What Separates Leaders from Followers

PwC identifies the critical distinction: organisations that take a ground-up, crowdsourced approach to AI rarely achieve transformation. The ones that do have senior leadership calling the shots — identifying the two or three workflows where AI fundamentally changes the economics, then applying the full weight of the organisation behind them.2 The tool is not the strategy. The strategic question is: which workflows will I rebuild from scratch?

For a senior executive with 25 years of business analytics and decision science experience, the next leap is less about personal capability and more about institutional impact. You already have the framework, the judgment, and the method. The frontier question is: are you deploying that capability into how your organisation thinks, plans, and decides — or is it still a personal practice?

"The companies that will dominate are not those that merely adopt AI, but those whose leaders cultivate an environment where AI augments human capability and becomes a force multiplier for team performance. This kind of transformation must be driven by the CEO because these shifts cut directly across existing structures, incentives, and power dynamics." — BCG, April 20261
