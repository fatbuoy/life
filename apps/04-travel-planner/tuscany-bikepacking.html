The journey

No conquering, just savouring

After completing the Via Alpina, time to give the boots a rest and get on the wheels for a gentle roll. This is the tentative plan for a 15-day two together bikepacking tour through the most iconic corners of Tuscany, averaging 50–60 km per day. The riding is rolling rather than mountainous, leaving room every day to wander a piazza, linger over lunch, or simply watch the mist lift off the vineyards.

Typical Sahani style, this is not an endurance challenge, more about one of Italy's most beautiful regions experienced at an unhurried pace, I might even get into the wine.

The idea behind the route

Not knowing much about the region, I looked into a few famous routes, the Strade Bianche, the Eroica, etc... However, the objective is to see more of Tuscany rather than ticking a box on Strava, so this is an attempt to combine Tuscany's most beautiful landscapes with its best towns. The white gravel roads and segments of the famous routes are a happy coincidence.

Reality check

Rolling hills, not Alpine climbs

Around 10,000 m of climbing sounds bigger than it is, so this is not a comparison to Via Alpina. Most climbs are over within a few kilometres, with no long sustained ascents.

  • 5–8% on most climbs
  • Occasional short ramps of 10–12%
  • Very few long continuous ascents

The spirit of the trip

Our kind of adventure.

Since our first proper bikepacking trip in Andalucia, this has been on our minds. Unlike the Via Alpina, this is not an expedition measured in passes conquered and kilometres banked, but a journey where the point is the place itself.

We'll be riding through vineyards and cypress-lined roads, stopping in medieval hill towns, eating what's seasonal, and staying in agriturismos and family-run B&Bs. We want to arrive each evening having genuinely seen where we rode through, not merely passed it.
