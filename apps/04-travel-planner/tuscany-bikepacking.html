The journey

Not about conquering, but savouring

After completing the Via Alpina, we're taking our bikes somewhere altogether different. This is our plan for a 15-day self-supported bikepacking tour through the most iconic corners of Tuscany, averaging 50–60 km per day. The riding is rolling rather than mountainous, leaving room every day to wander a piazza, linger over lunch, or simply watch the mist lift off the vineyards.

Less about endurance, more about one of Italy's most beautiful regions experienced at an unhurried pace. Exactly what we're after.

How hard is it?

Rolling hills, not Alpine climbs

Around 10,000 m of climbing sounds significant, but the hills here are nothing like the sustained Alpine ascents. Most are over within a few kilometres.

  • 5–8% on most climbs
  • Occasional short ramps of 10–12%
  • Very few long continuous ascents

The spirit of the trip

Slow down. Enjoy.

Unlike an expedition where every day is measured by distance covered, this journey is designed to let Tuscany reveal itself gradually: vineyards in the morning mist, espresso in a piazza no one else seems to have found, a glass of Brunello in the golden hour before dinner.

The aim is to stay in agriturismos and family-run B&Bs, eat what's seasonal, and arrive each evening having genuinely seen where we rode through, not merely passed it.

The perfect contrast to the Via Alpina.
